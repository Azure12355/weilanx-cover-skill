# Visual style guide

This guide defines the portable defaults for the skill. A user's explicit direction or supplied style reference takes precedence.

## Stable defaults

- Use a strict 3:4 vertical canvas by default, or the user's exact requested ratio.
- Keep the hierarchy clear: main title first, person second, core topic card or graphic third.
- Use extra-bold Chinese display typography with a thick black stroke, warm-white outer keyline, and short hard shadow. Limit the title to three colors and use one color per line.
- Preserve the person's original photographic identity and texture. Treat the portrait as a clean cutout; do not redraw or style-transfer the face, hair, skin, hands, or clothing.
- Use dark cards, white outlines, short shadows, and restrained accent colors. The result should feel like an editorial content poster rather than an application dashboard.

## Background

Prefer deep navy, indigo, aubergine, charcoal purple, or dark blue-purple over large pure-black areas. Build depth with low-contrast, topic-specific silhouettes, paths, nodes, grids, dots, contour lines, clouds, paper grain, or subtle particles.

Background elements must stay behind the subject, title, cards, and small labels. Do not reuse stars, grids, or code fragments when they do not fit the topic.

## Composition options

Choose one direction that fits the topic:

- asymmetric information collage
- card matrix
- path or process flow
- central topic card
- three-step tutorial
- tool-and-file collage
- side-by-side comparison
- oversized-title poster

Across a batch, vary both the main composition and the placement of supporting elements.

## Aspect-ratio adaptation

- Compose natively for each ratio; never treat a crop as the finished adaptation.
- For 3:4, stack the title and supporting cards vertically while keeping the subject large enough for mobile thumbnails.
- For 4:3, balance the title and subject across two broad columns, with cards below or through the center.
- For 16:9, use a strong left-right split and keep all important content inside thumbnail-safe margins.
- For 2.35:1, reduce vertical stacking, keep the title compact, and run cards or process steps horizontally.
- Preserve the same hierarchy, exact title, subject identity, and background language across a multi-ratio set.

## Avoid

- Cyberpunk, esports, HUD, holograms, neon, glassmorphism, black chrome, metallic frames, and black-and-gold styling.
- Rainbow title lettering, intense single-color light washes, gold gradients, and excessive glow.
- Large empty black areas, noisy high-contrast backgrounds, scrapbook styling, torn-paper decoration, and generic office-template layouts.
- Extra people, face swaps, tiny portraits, clothing or pose changes, and colored outlines around the person.
- Geometry, engraving, crosshatching, paper, canvas, plastic, or smeared textures on the face, hair, hands, or clothing.
- Fabricated views, likes, search volume, users, rankings, revenue, timestamps, watermarks, or unrelated text.
